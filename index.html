04 / UNDERSTAND THE MODEL

Simple rules. Explicit limits.

A transparent baseline for questions about learning, inheritance, and organization.

01

The world

A grid of renewable locations has three habitat types. Each habitat favors one of three harvesting actions. Agents see their local habitat and resources, but must learn which action works.

At the scheduled change, each habitat favors a new action. The population keeps its existing memories.

02

The agent

A small learning policy tracks the success of each action in each habitat. It usually chooses its best estimate and sometimes explores. Success earns energy; activity costs energy.

Agents reproduce into vacant neighboring locations when they have enough energy. They die when energy runs out or after 50 ticks.

03

What is inherited?

Faithful descendants receive a copy of their parent’s learned estimates. The no-inheritance control starts with neutral estimates. Shuffled inheritance preserves the entries but permutes their action labels.

Optional policy mutation is available in the live world. It is disabled in the first controlled research protocol.

04

What does this establish?

Results describe this artificial ecology. Agents are baseline learning algorithms, not language models. A pattern here does not establish a fact about real LLMs, biological evolution, or real-world replication.

Meaningful specialization and open-ended complexity remain research questions; this version does not claim to have demonstrated them.

The first protocol

The primary outcome is the average fraction of occupied locations during ticks 120–239. Extinct runs contribute zero for the remaining horizon. The primary contrast asks whether the faithful-memory advantage over no inheritance becomes smaller after environmental change.

24 exploration seeds and 48 separate confirmation seeds each generate all six conditions. Uncertainty uses 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples of independent seeds. Secondary comparisons are descriptive. Confirmation applies only within this model and protocol.

World configurations, source hashes, raw action logs, and negative results are preserved. A source change creates a new campaign identity; existing trial records are never silently updated.
