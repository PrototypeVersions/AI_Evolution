START HERE / THE RULES IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

Keep two. Replace two. Learn again.

The successful models keep learning.
Only two replacement models are created.

What does a model learn?

Each model is a small neural network with 512 adjustable numbers, called weights and biases. It practices tasks on strings of eight 0s and 1s, such as reversing their order. Training adjusts those numbers to make its answers more accurate.

What is a “random restart”?

Here it means replacing a model with a new, untrained one: random connection weights, zero biases, and no learned weights from a parent. The two survivors are not reset. They keep their numbers and continue training.

Choose an approach to see what happens in one round.

Illustration of the rules, not a recorded round. A–F are example model names; A and B are selected in this example.

Why can the orange line keep rising?

Because its survivors keep learning. An original model can stay for many rounds while untrained replacements come and go. A rising score does not show that random restarts helped. It also does not show knowledge passing into the fresh models.

Why four models, and why keep two?

Keeping two preserves useful learned states; replacing two gives alternative starting points a chance. Four models and half-population replacement are small, inexpensive starting choices—not proven optimal settings. Both populations use the same rule, so their main difference is whether replacements inherit learned weights.

Each approach gets 2,048 training examples per round: 512 per model in a population, or all 2,048 for the single learner. Each comparison is repeated with three random seeds: (4 + 4 + 1) × 3 = 27 active models. The learning and evaluation compute ledger is matched; memory and total runtime are not.

To establish whether replacement itself helps, a later study should also compare against four models that keep training without replacement. This chapter does not include that control.

How to read the graph

Across: training rounds

One round gives each approach 2,048 training examples. A survivor keeps its weights between rounds. The background task emphasis changes every 24 rounds.

Up: correct output bits

On the default view, 7 correct bits out of 8 is 87.5%. “Complete answers” requires all eight bits to be right. These are scores on these tasks, not a general intelligence scale.

Lines: selected performers

Each line averages three independent runs. The shaded band on the default view spans their lowest and highest scores; it is not a confidence interval. Separate selection problems decide which model is plotted.

03 / WHAT THE RECORD SUPPORTS

Evidence before a story.

AUTOMATIC MEASUREMENTS · NO LLM COMMENTARY

THE CURRENT COMPARISON

Three paired seeds. These are descriptive differences, not significance tests. The population–single comparison changes several mechanisms together.

THE WARM-START ASSAY

Every 24 rounds, disposable copies get 256 identical training examples: one copy keeps its learned weights, one starts fresh. This tests retained learning, not whether evolution is better.

04 / THE EXPERIMENT IS INSPECTABLE

What counts. What doesn’t.

Read protocol 1 ↗
01

Learning happens in the weights.

All models learn by adjusting weights. Both populations retain two trained survivors. Only the inherited-copy approach gives replacements their parents’ learned weights. Every model has the same fixed architecture.

02

Evaluation has its own inputs.

Training, parent selection and reporting use disjoint input strings. Reporting scores never influence reproduction. Untrained combinations are an exploratory probe.

03

Computation has a ledger.

Training, selection and audits all count. Learning and evaluation budgets are equal across arms. Viewer preparation, total runtime and memory use are accounted separately.

04

A chapter can reach its limit.

Four curriculum cycles, 384 rounds. Then training stops and the checkpoint is preserved for review. This finite study cannot demonstrate unlimited intelligence.
